1. Epic overview

Epic 4 handles the money and the insight: calculating what each farmer is paid from the graded quantity, applying authorised deductions, approvals and payouts, recording buyer payments against invoices, issuing receipts, tracking outstanding amounts, and giving the Manager reports, performance analysis, forecasts and exports.

Stakeholders: Finance Officer, Farmer (payment transparency), Manager (management), Payment service providers (secondary).

2. Core concepts

2.1 How a farmer payment is calculated (E4-US1)

gross = accepted_qty_kg × price_per_kg (price from the ACTIVE price list for crop + grade + centre) net = gross − total_deductions Example: 118.5 kg × LKR 160 = LKR 18,960.00 gross; deduction "transport LKR 500" → net LKR 18,460.00
  • The inputs come from the approved inspection (accepted quantity and grade) – the finance officer cannot type a quantity. This links quality to money.
  • The price comes from get_current_price() (the active price for that crop and grade, set by an administrator or finance officer in Price Management). A finance officer may override the price only with a written justification note.
  • Calculating again is allowed only while the payment is still calculated (not yet approved or paid).

2.2 Payment life-cycle and separation of duties

calculated ──approve──► approved ──pay──► paid (receipt issued) └────reject (reason)────► rejected
  • Maker–checker (separation of duties): a system setting require_payment_approval_separation can force that the person who calculated a payment cannot also approve it (403). Administrators are exempt.
  • Payout needs approval: pay_farmer_payment() refuses to pay anything that is not approved or is already paid.
  • When paid, a numbered payout receipt is created (RCT-…); the farmer can print it.

2.3 Buyer payments, partial payments and receipts (E4-US4 / US5)

  • An invoice has total, amount paid and balance. Buyers may pay in instalments; each payment creates its own numbered receipt and the invoice becomes paid when the balance reaches zero.
  • record_buyer_payment() works inside one transaction, so the invoice, payment row and receipt always agree.
  • Outstanding payments: receivables (buyers owe us) and payables (we owe farmers) with days overdue so finance can chase them.

3.5 Management reports and export – E4-US6 to US9

  • Report and export endpoints allow manager and finance officer (inventory manager only for exports); other roles → 403.
  • Date range from / to is parsed with defaults; the export type must be one of the supported report names (otherwise 404 "Unknown report type").
  • CSV formula-injection protection: any cell that starts with =, +, - or @ is prefixed so Excel cannot execute it as a formula; commas and quotes are escaped. This is covered by an automated test.
  • PDF export uses the browser print engine on the on-screen report, so what you see is what is exported.

3.6 Price management (feeds payments)

  • Only the administrator or finance officer creates or changes prices; the active price for a crop/grade/centre is resolved by get_current_price().
  • A price has a status (active / inactive / superseded) so history stays for the price-trend report.

4. Suggested live demo

  1. Finance officer → Calculate payment for an inspected collection: show quantity and grade price pulled automatically. Add a deduction larger than the gross → refused; then a valid "transport" deduction (US1, US2).
  2. Approve & disburse: approve, then mark paid; try paying an unapproved payment to show the rule.
  3. Farmer → My payments: statuses and the printable payout receipt (US3).
  4. Finance → Buyer invoices: record a partial payment, show the receipt and the balance; then try to overpay → refused; pay the balance → invoice becomes paid (US4).
  5. Outstanding payments: receivables, payables and days overdue (US5).
  6. Manager login → Management reports: Operations, Performance (farmer / buyer / waste), Financial with profit estimate, supply-demand forecast and price trends (US6–US8). Show there are no write buttons.
  7. Export CSV and PDF (US9).

5. Likely viva questions and model answers

Q1. How is the farmer payment calculated?

Accepted quantity from the approved inspection × current purchase price for the crop and grade = gross; minus authorised deductions = net. The finance officer cannot alter the quantity; a price override needs a written note.

Q2. What stops finance from paying the wrong amount?

Quantity and grade come from the inspection, price from the price list, deductions are validated (positive, described, not more than gross), and the amount is rounded to 2 decimals and stored on the payment.

Q3. What is separation of duties and do you have it?

The person who prepares a payment should not approve it. A system setting can enforce that the approver differs from the calculator (403 otherwise).

Q4. Why must a payment be approved before payout?

Approval is a control point. The payout database function refuses anything not approved and refuses a second payment, so double payment is impossible.

Q5. How do partial buyer payments work?

Each payment reduces the invoice balance and creates its own receipt. The function rejects amounts above the balance and payments on paid or cancelled invoices; when balance reaches zero the invoice becomes paid.

Q6. Why are payments done in database functions?

Invoice update, payment row and receipt must succeed or fail together; a transaction guarantees consistent financial records.

Q7. What are outstanding payments?

Receivables = unpaid invoices from buyers, payables = approved farmer payments not yet paid, each with days overdue.

Q8. How is the profit estimate computed?

Revenue from buyer sales minus farmer payouts minus wastage cost. It is an estimate because other operating costs are not recorded.

Q9. How does the forecast work?

A least-squares straight line is fitted to the monthly supply and demand history and extended for three months. The screen states the method.

Q10. What can the Manager do?

View reports and orders and export them. The role is read-only, enforced on every write endpoint with 403.

Q11. How do you protect exported CSV files?

Cells starting with = + - or @ are neutralised (formula-injection protection) and the data is escaped; export is limited to authorised roles.

Q12. How does a farmer know their payment status?

My payments lists calculated / approved / paid / rejected with amounts and a printable receipt once paid.

Q13. Where do prices come from?

The administrator or finance officer maintains the price list per crop, grade and centre; payments and the marketplace read the active price.

6. Know your limitations

  • No real payment-gateway or bank integration: payments are recorded by finance staff after money moves outside the system (the Sprint 0 budget includes a payment module as a future step).
  • The profit estimate ignores overheads (salaries, transport, rent) because they are not captured.
  • Forecasting is a linear trend, not seasonal or machine-learning based.
  • Email notifications of payments depend on an SMTP server being configured; otherwise only in-app notifications are sent.
  • PDF export relies on the browser print dialog instead of a server-side PDF generator.

8. Appendix – system foundations (same in every guide)

8.1 Architecture in one picture

Browser (React 18 + Vite + Tailwind + TanStack Query + react-hook-form + Zod) | HTTPS, JSON, header Authorization: Bearer <JWT> v Express REST API (Node.js + TypeScript) helmet · CORS · rate limit · authenticate · requireRole · validate(Zod) · controller · errorHandler | supabase-js (service-role key, server side only) v Supabase: PostgreSQL (tables, constraints, RPC functions, triggers) · Auth · Storage (private buckets)

Layered design: presentation (React pages) → API/business layer (Express controllers and services) → data layer (Supabase/PostgreSQL). Business rules that must never be bypassed (stock, weights, payments) are enforced in the database through PostgreSQL functions, so even a buggy client cannot break them.

8.2 Authentication vs authorisation

ConceptWhat it answersHow HarvestHub does it
Authentication"Who are you?"Email + password are checked by Supabase Auth. On success the API returns a JWT access token and a refresh token. The frontend stores them and the Axios interceptor adds Authorization: Bearer <token> to every request; on a 401 it tries the refresh token once, otherwise logs the user out.
Authorisation (RBAC)"What may you do?"authenticate middleware verifies the token with Supabase, loads the user's profiles row (role, account_status) and rejects suspended / inactive / pending accounts. requireRole(...) then allows only listed roles (administrator is a superuser). Wrong role → 403.
Ownership checks"Is this YOUR record?"Role alone is not enough: a farmer must not read another farmer's data. Controllers call helpers such as assertFarmerAccess / assertBuyerAccess (services/access.ts) → 403 if the record belongs to someone else.
Row Level Security (RLS)DB-level safety netPolicies are enabled on tables in Supabase. The API itself uses the service-role key, so the API-layer checks above are the primary control; RLS protects direct database access.

The nine roles are: farmer, collection_centre_officer, quality_inspector, inventory_manager, buyer, finance_officer, transport_coordinator, manager, administrator. Only farmer and buyer can self-register; staff accounts are created by an administrator.

8.5 Cross-cutting security measures

  • helmet security headers; CORS restricted to the configured frontend origin in production.
  • Rate limiting with express-rate-limit (general API and a stricter limit on /api/auth).
  • Safe search: user search text is stripped of characters that could alter a PostgREST filter (, ( ) % * \) and limited to 60 characters.
  • Mass-assignment protection: update endpoints copy only a whitelist of columns (a farmer cannot set their own verification status).
  • Generic login error ("Invalid email or password") so attackers cannot discover which emails exist.
  • Secrets only in .env (service-role key never reaches the browser); private storage buckets with short-lived signed URLs for documents.
  • Notifications and audit: important actions create in-app notification rows (and emails when SMTP is configured); auction awards and rejections are written to the audit log.
